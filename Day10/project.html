A.P.J Abdul Kalam

Missile Man of India

The Missile Man of India is the nickname for Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam, a renowned scientist and the 11th President of India (2002-2007). He earned this title for his pivotal role in India's missile and nuclear weapons programs, particularly his work at the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) and Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO).

BIOGRAPHY

Early LIfe and Education

  • Born Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam on October 15, 1931, in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, India.
  • He came from a modest Muslim family; his father owned a boat and his mother was a homemaker.
  • Despite facing financial hardship, Kalam excelled in his studies, eventually earning a degree in aeronautical engineering from the Madras Institute of Technology.

Career as Scientist

  • Kalam joined the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) in 1958.
  • He moved to the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) in 1969, becoming the project director for SLV-III, the first Indian-designed and produced satellite launch vehicle.
  • He played a significant role in India's missile development and was involved in the 1998 Pokhran-II nuclear tests.

Presidency and Later Life

  • Kalam was elected the 11th President of India in 2002.
  • He was known for his engagement with the public, particularly the youth, which led to him being called the "People's President
  • After his presidency, he returned to teaching, writing, and public service
  • He passed away on July 27, 2015, in Shillong, India.
